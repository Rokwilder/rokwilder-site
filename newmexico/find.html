How New Mexico's draw works

  • There are no points. The Department does not grant preference points or bonus points to someone who was not drawn. Every application gets a random sequence number. The computer then looks at that whole application, first choice, then second, then third, before it moves to the next application. A second or third choice can be filled before another hunter's first choice for the same hunt.
  • First choice is the figure on this page. The rate is first-choice licenses drawn divided by first-choice applicants in the same pool. The Department's odds page says applicant totals can overstate competition, because a person who draws an earlier choice is no longer in the later hunts. The rate is not a guaranteed chance in a future draw.
  • Three pools. State law sets a minimum of 84 percent of draw licenses for New Mexico residents, 10 percent for residents and nonresidents who applied with a registered outfitter, and 6 percent for nonresidents who applied without an outfitter. The shares are not always exact, especially when the outfitter pool is thin. This page uses the licenses the 2026 report issued to each pool. It does not recompute 84, 10, and 6.
  • Resident-only hunts. Antlerless elk licenses, and hunts held only on a State Game Commission wildlife management area, go to New Mexico residents. A hunt that includes a wildlife management area and other land is not marked resident-only. A nonresident result is shown only when the report has applicants in that pool.
  • Small quotas. The 2026-27 draw FAQ says a nonresident is extremely unlikely to draw a hunt code with 12 or fewer licenses, and an outfitter-pool applicant is unlikely to draw a hunt code with 6 or fewer licenses. A resident who applies with a nonresident is limited by the nonresident pool.
  • Fourth choice, deer and elk only. After every application is examined, leftover deer and elk licenses can go to people who marked a fourth choice. That choice is a quadrant of the state, not a hunt code. There is no refund if you do not like the hunt you are assigned. An elk hunter can be assigned an antlerless leftover even if the first three choices were bull hunts. The 2026 complete report shows 884 licenses issued on a fourth choice. Those draws are not the first-choice rate.
  • Hunt codes through 2026-27. Three letters for the species, a middle digit for the sporting arm (1 any legal, 2 bow, 3 muzzleloader or bow), then the hunt number. In 2027-28 the Department is changing to a descriptive code. Its April 10, 2026 explainer uses ELK-2-264 becoming ELK-15-B1-ES-PUB as an example of the format. That example is not a 2027-28 hunt table. The tables are not published yet.
  • Bighorn sheep. The 2026 odds are for the hunt code, not for one area. The hunter also picks hunt areas and dates under that code. This page does not invent area-level odds.
  • Parties. Each hunter applies and pays, then attaches. The maximum is 1 for bighorn and bear, 2 for turkey, oryx, and ibex, and 4 for pronghorn, deer, elk, barbary sheep, and javelina. The odds workbooks do not say which applications were parties, so this page does not adjust for a party.
  • 2026-27 deadlines, already past. Bear and turkey draw permits: apply by February 11, 2026, results February 18, 2026. Barbary sheep, bighorn, deer, elk, ibex, javelina, pronghorn, oryx, and private-land-only deer in units 2A, 2B, 2C, 4, and 5A: apply by March 18, 2026, results April 22, 2026. Applications were due by 5:00 p.m. Mountain Time. 2027-28 deadlines are not listed here.

Landowner tags, the outfitter pool, and private land

  • The outfitter pool is part of the public draw. It is the 10 percent share for people who already had a contract with a registered New Mexico outfitter when they applied. Drawing in the nonresident pool does not stop a hunter from hiring an outfitter later. The outfitter pool is not EPLUS.
  • EPLUS is private-land elk. It is not in the drawing-odds workbook. In the primary zone, a landowner gets authorizations that a hunter uses to buy a private-land elk license. A unit-wide authorization is valid on that ranch, on other unit-wide ranches in the same unit, and on legally accessible public land in the same unit. A ranch-only authorization is limited to the enrolled ranch. No public land may be hunted on a ranch-only license. In units 4 and 5A, and in the secondary and special zones, a ranch-only elk license may also be used on other private land in the same unit and zone with written permission.
  • Special zone and secondary zone. The special zone is unit 55A and unit 54 west of NM 199. Those authorizations are ranch-only. The secondary zone issues a ranch code, not an authorization. The hunter then buys an over-the-counter private-land elk license. The Department does not publish a secondary-zone landowner list.
  • Unit-wide seasons follow the public draw. The 2026-27 booklet says unit-wide dates match the public-draw hunt for the same unit, bag, and sporting arm. Ranch-only dates are printed separately in the booklet. A hunter may not hold both a private-land elk license and a draw elk license in the same license year.
  • The Department is not the broker. Any price or permission between a hunter and a landowner is private. The Department says it is not a party to those arrangements and does not resolve those disputes. The current landowner lists and the unit-wide ranch map are on the EPLUS page.
  • Other private-land licenses. Private-land deer is over the counter, except in units 2A, 2B, 2C, 4, and 5A, where it is a draw hunt and needs a landowner authorization. Private-land pronghorn has been over the counter since the 2019-20 license year. The 2026-27 booklet adds license limits in units 41, 42, 47, 56, 57, 58, and 59. Private-land oryx is also over the counter. This list does not invent odds for those licenses.

Over the counter, outside this draw list

  • Bear. Most bear hunting is over the counter by bear management zone, with a total limit and a female limit. The draw list is the small set of entry hunts (wildlife management areas, Valle Vidal, Sugarite, and a youth hunt). A bear license must be purchased at least two calendar days before hunting. A successful draw applicant still buys the over-the-counter bear license. The 2026 bear workbook did not publish how many of those permits were drawn on the first choice, so those hunts have no result band.
  • Turkey. Spring and fall seasons are over the counter in open areas. Drawn permits cover some wildlife management areas, Valle Vidal, Valles Caldera, and similar hunts. A successful applicant still buys an over-the-counter turkey license. First-choice drawn counts were not published for 2026.
  • Barbary sheep, javelina, and ibex. There are over-the-counter licenses besides the draw hunts. Public and private barbary licenses are over the counter except in draw-only or restricted areas, including McGregor Range, White Sands, and Fort Bliss. Javelina has an over-the-counter season outside the listed draw units. Ibex draw hunts are in the Florida Mountains (unit 25). The year-round over-the-counter hunt IBX-1-528 is valid statewide off the Florida Mountains. This page does not give those over-the-counter hunts a draw band.
  • Not in this list. Cougar is over the counter by zone. Enhancement, auction, raffle, and population-management hunts are not in the 2026 public-draw workbook used here. Pheasant and sandhill crane draws are not in this finder.

How a word is chosen. For a pool with first-choice applicants, every applicant drawing is "All drew." At least three out of four is "Most drew." At least one out of four is "Some drew." At least one out of twenty is "Few drew." Below that, with a published rate, the hunt stays off the list. A pool with no first-choice applicants is left blank. It is not treated as zero.
