How Montana's limited draw works

  • Bonus points are extra chances, then a random draw. FWP's bonus-point page says every application gets one chance. Points are squared, then one is added for this year's application. Their example: 10 bonus points is (10 x 10) + 1 = 101 chances. More chances are not a place in line. Someone with zero points can still draw ahead of someone with many.
  • These are not preference points. Preference points are only for nonresident combination licenses, on the other button. Residents do not use preference points. The maximum preference points a person may hold is 3. Bonus points have no legal maximum. The stepper on this page stops at the highest total in the 2026 tables for that species.
  • First choice only. Bonus points are not used in second-choice or third-choice drawings. You lose bonus points if you are successful on your first choice, or if hunting privileges are revoked. Drawing a second or third choice does not reset them. A level with no row in the report is left blank. It is not treated as zero, and it is not filled in from the level next to it.
  • Residents and nonresidents are separate. Nonresidents are limited to, but not guaranteed, 10 percent of a drawing quota. That cap includes nonresident landowners. Landowner preference is drawn first. If every resident who applied is successful, nonresidents may be given more than 10 percent. This page uses the residency row from the bonus-point report. It does not recompute the cap. Landowner rows are not the resident odds.
  • For moose, sheep, and goat districts with a quota under 10, the 2026 regulations say the nonresident chance is calculated from a regional pool and the districts rotate. The nonresident row in the report is what is shown.
  • Parties. Up to 5 people may apply together for some drawings. The bonus-point tables do not say which rows were parties, so this page does not adjust for a party.
  • 2026 dates, not 2027. Deer and elk permits and nonresident combination licenses: apply by April 1, 2026. Moose, sheep, goat, and bison: May 1, 2026. Deer B, elk B, and antelope: June 1, 2026. Some B licenses are sold over the counter beginning June 15, 2026. SuperTags are a separate raffle with a June 30 deadline and are not in this list. FWP had not posted 2027 application dates when this page was built.
  • A permit is not the same as the general license. Residents buy a general deer license and a general elk license over the counter. A limited permit is used with that general license and can restrict where an antlered animal may be taken. The 2026 regulations say a general license may still be used in an open district outside the dates on the permit, and antlerless elk hunting is not limited by an either-sex or brow-tined bull permit. Those general seasons are the Resident general licenses button.
  • Bison. The 2026 catalog has drawing statistics and no bison bonus-point report. The three bison licenses are shown without a point ladder. The bison hunt roster (codes 385-77 and 395-77) is a sign-up list, not this drawing, and it is not given a band.

How a word is chosen. For each printed bonus-point level with a first-choice row, every applicant drawing is "All drew." At least three out of four is "Most drew." At least one out of four is "Some drew." At least one out of twenty is "Few drew." Below that, the hunt stays off the list. A quota marked UNL in the drawing statistics is labeled Unlimited when everyone in that row was drawn. Nothing here is an exact Montana percentage. The free page shows how the bands work. The full list, and the exact counts where FWP published them, is the Full Pass.
