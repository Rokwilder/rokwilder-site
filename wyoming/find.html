Wyoming draw rules to know

  • Nonresidents, elk, deer and pronghorn: Chapter 44 puts at least 75% of the special and regular nonresident licenses in the preference-point draw (most points first) and up to 25% in a random draw. If you don't draw in the point draw, you go into that draw's random draw. Points only count on your 1st choice.
  • Special vs. regular (nonresidents): after nonresident landowner licenses, Chapter 44 puts 40% of the remaining nonresident elk, deer and antelope licenses in the special draw and 60% in the regular draw. Special licenses cost more. Each draw has its own point pool and random pool, so the verdicts differ. Reduced-price cow/calf and doe/fawn licenses are not in the special draw.
  • Residents: the resident elk, deer and pronghorn draws are random. Residents don't use preference points for these species. The verdict is an estimate from the quota and 1st-choice applicants, not a percentage WGFD prints.
  • Moose and bighorn sheep: residents and nonresidents both use preference points. WGFD's preference-points page says 75% of licenses in a hunt area and license type go to the point draw and 25% to a random draw.
  • Mountain goat and bison: random draw only, no preference points. The verdict is the same kind of estimate.
  • Nonresidents in wilderness: WGFD's FAQ says a nonresident hunting big or trophy game in a designated wilderness area needs a licensed guide or a resident companion with the free non-commercial guide license (W.S. 23-2-401). The law does not say that about hiking, fishing or small game. Check whether your hunt area includes wilderness before you apply.
  • Application dates posted for 2026 (these windows have passed; WGFD has not posted 2027 dates, and this page does not guess them): nonresident elk Jan. 2 to Feb. 2; moose, bighorn sheep and mountain goat Jan. 2 to Apr. 30; bison Mar. 2 to Apr. 30; deer, pronghorn and resident elk Jan. 2 to June 1. Preference-point-only purchase: the application dates table says July 1 to Nov. 2, while Chapter 44 and the preference-points page say July through October 31. Check WGFD before you apply.

How a verdict is figured. Where WGFD prints 1st-choice preference-point odds, those odds are used as printed. The random draw is an estimate: licenses in that report divided by 1st-choice applicants, capped at 100%. A point holder's verdict combines them. Resident elk, deer and pronghorn, cow/calf, doe/fawn, mountain goat and bison use the random estimate only. The words are bands, not exact percentages: about 100% is "Yes, you'll draw"; 75% and up is Likely; 25% and up is Maybe; 5% and up is Long shot; under 5% is left off the list. Quotas and applicant numbers change every year. Not legal advice. Always check the current WGFD regulations.
