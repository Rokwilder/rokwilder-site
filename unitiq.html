What's in a UnitIQ package

One unit, one species, every season. Built from public data and labeled for what it is: an estimate to help you scout.

Estimated hotspots

Ranked zones scored on range, cover, water, terrain and distance from roads. Every zone is labeled as an estimate.

Migration corridors and timing

Mapped corridors and concentration areas, with notes on where animals are likely to be during archery, muzzleloader and each rifle season.

Water

Springs, perennial streams, ponds and tanks from USGS hydrography, so you know where water sits relative to cover.

Transition areas

Feed-to-cover edges, saddles, benches and funnels where terrain or vegetation pinches movement.

Public land around private land

Public ground near private fields, with a legal-access check on every zone: reachable, check access, or landlocked.

GPX/KML pins

Every zone as a waypoint with lat/long and notes. Import into onX, Gaia, or Google Earth.

What you get

  • PDF report (about 12 pages): unit overview, season-by-season breakdown, zone table, access notes
  • Interactive web map with a season selector
  • GPX and KML waypoint files
  • Deer and elk are separate packages

What it isn't

It's not a guarantee you'll see animals, and it's not a guide. It's a researched starting point so your scouting days go to the most likely ground first. Always confirm property lines and road status in the field.
