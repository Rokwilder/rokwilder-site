UnitIQ Scouting Packages are limited per unit and per season, so we don't send too many hunters to the same spots. The limit follows the official tag numbers for that unit and species: 1 package for every 25 tags, at least 3 and at most 30 for that unit that season. Over-the-counter and unlimited-tag units are a flat 30 per unit per season. An extra-unit add-on counts toward the cap of that extra unit. In larger units, buyers get a varied mix of spots so the same spots aren't handed to everyone. The Full Pass does not count. If a unit sells out after you pay, we refund that purchase in full.

Scouting packages

UnitIQ Scouting Packages and extra units are made for the unit, species and season you choose. Boots-on-the-Ground

For Boots-on-the-Ground, we go to your unit on our own before your season, scout it, and send you a field report, photos and updated waypoints. It's information only.

We don't guide, lead or go with you in the field. We don't hunt with you, take you hunting, transport you, your gear or game, set up camp, provide food, or pack out game. We don't meet you in the field during your hunt or help you take an animal.

  • Where: Boots-on-the-Ground is open to requests in Colorado only for now. Utah and Arizona are coming once licensed: we'll only offer it in those states once any required state license or permit is in place. Until then, we don't take requests or payments for Utah or Arizona.
  • No drones. We never use drones to look for or scout wildlife. In Colorado that's against the law.
  • Game cameras: we only use cameras that store photos on the camera, never ones that send photos live, and only where state rules and the land manager allow.
  • Price and dates: the total depends on the unit, the travel distance and the number of days. We agree on the total, the dates and what's included in writing before you pay.
  • Conditions change: animals move. A spot that held animals when we scouted it may be empty when you hunt.
  • Refunds: Boots-on-the-Ground can be refunded in full within 72 hours of purchase, as long as we haven't started traveling or scouting for your hunt. After that it can't be refunded, unless we cancel or weather, fire or a closure stops the scout; the refund policy explains those cases. Once we've started, you still get the field report and waypoints for whatever we scouted.

Estimates, not guarantees

Draw odds, harvest success, point projections, hotspot maps, spots and waypoints are estimates. Draw odds and harvest data come from public reports by Colorado Parks and Wildlife, the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources and the Arizona Game and Fish Department. Past results don't guarantee that you'll draw a tag or harvest an animal. Spots and waypoints are suggestions, not guarantees that animals will be there.

Conditions change: weather, fire, roads, closures, land ownership and animal movement all change from year to year and day to day. Data can be wrong or out of date, and answers from the AI hunt advisor can be wrong or incomplete. Always check the state's official regulations, deadlines and reports before you apply or hunt. We aren't responsible for points, application fees or tags you spend or lose based on our estimates.

Your responsibilities

You alone are responsible for knowing and following all hunting laws and regulations, including licensing, season dates, unit boundaries, weapon rules, and rules for game cameras and other technology. A few examples, so you know they exist:

  • Colorado makes it illegal to use a drone to look for, scout or detect wildlife as an aid in hunting, and limits cameras that send photos live.
  • Utah bans trail cameras on public land from July 31 to December 31 and bans buying or selling trail camera photos or data to help take big game.
  • Arizona doesn't allow trail cameras or their images to be used to help take wildlife, or drones to help locate wildlife during an open season.

Rules change, so check the current regulations yourself. You're also responsible for knowing who owns the land and what the access rules are. A spot, route or waypoint on our maps doesn't mean you're allowed to be there. Check land ownership, closures and signs yourself, and get permission before you go on private land. Don't use our information to trespass or to harass wildlife.
