Utah extended archery 2026

Still have an unfilled archery buck deer or bull elk tag when your hunt ends? Utah lets you keep hunting with a bow in its extended archery areas, on later season dates. You don’t need to apply for another permit. You do need to take a short online course first.

Does my permit qualify?

As long as you obtain an archery buck deer or bull elk permit in 2026—and you complete the required extended archery ethics course—you may also hunt any of Utah’s extended archery areas during the seasons listed below.Utah DWR, 2026 Utah Field Regulations Guidebook, p. 8
Yes: a general-season archery buck deer permit, if you haven’t taken a deer by the time your archery buck hunt ends.
Yes: a general-season archery bull elk permit, if you haven’t taken an elk by the time your archery hunt ends.
Yes: limited-entry archery buck deer and bull elk hunters who don’t harvest during the limited-entry season. You still must finish your harvest survey within 30 days of the end of the season on your permit.
Yes: an “extended archery only” general-season buck deer permit (hunt DB0008) from the big game drawing. It is good only in the extended archery areas, during the extended archery deer dates.
Yes: youth general-season deer permits (archery, early or late any legal weapon) and the multiseason spike bull elk permit also list the extended archery areas, archery only.
No: antlerless permits and HAMSS (handgun-archery-muzzleloader-shotgun-straight walled rifle-only) permits.
Important: Check the hunt number (not just the unit name) on your permit carefully and refer to the Utah Hunt Planner (hunt.utah.gov) to make sure your permit allows you to hunt in an extended archery unit within the allowed season dates.Utah DWR, 2026 Utah Field Regulations Guidebook, p. 8

What you can take

Deer: a deer of either sex (hunter’s choice), with archery equipment.
Elk: any bull elk or antlerless elk, even where an extended area overlaps a spike bull unit.
